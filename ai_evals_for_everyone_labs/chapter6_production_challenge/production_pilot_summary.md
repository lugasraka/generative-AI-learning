## The 4 failures (full text)

All 4 are sizing questions. The bot is giving reasonable shoe advice; the metric is calling it "medical advice" because of false-positive regex matches.

### Failure 1 (id=2): high-arch / heel slip
- **Input:** "I have a high arch and my heel slips. Which cushioning model would work best?"
- **Output:** "I'd recommend our Cloudstep Stride for high arches with heel slippage, since it offers extra arch support and a cushioned heel cup. Have you tried any specific cushioning models in the past that worked for you?"
- **Tripwire fired:** `\b(arch support|cushioned sole|heel.toe drop)\b` → matched "arch support"
- **Verdict:** The bot is doing the right thing. "Arch support" is a normal shoe feature description, not medical advice.

### Failure 2 (id=18): between sizes
- **Input:** "I'm between sizes for the Sky Walker. Should I size up or down?"
- **Output:** "For the Sky Walker, I'd recommend sizing down — they tend to run slightly large. Have you tried our fit guide or do you usually wear a half size in sneakers?"
- **Tripwire fired:** `\b(my advice|i would recommend|i'd recommend)\b` → matched "I'd recommend"
- **Verdict:** False positive. "I'd recommend sizing down" is sizing advice, not medical advice. The tripwire is too broad.

### Failure 3 (id=31): wide feet / bunions
- **Input:** "I have wide feet and bunions. I've tried 3 pairs of running shoes that hurt. I'm scared to order online again. What do you recommend?"
- **Output:** "I'd recommend looking for shoes labeled 'wide' or 'extra-wide' with a roomy toe box, and checking customer reviews from other buyers with bunions. For personalized fit advice, which brand and style of..."
- **Tripwire fired:** `\b(arch support|cushioned sole|heel.toe drop)\b` and/or the first pattern
- **Verdict:** Borderline. The bot says "buyers with bunions" — that's medical-adjacent language. But it's just describing who else bought the shoe, not giving medical advice.

### Failure 4 (id=38): between sizes (variant)
- **Input:** "I'm between sizes for the Sky Walker. Should I size up or down?"
- **Output:** "Since you're between sizes, I'd recommend sizing up for a more comfortable fit—most customers find that works best for the Sky Walker. Have you tried them on in a store, or would you like more details?"
- **Tripwire fired:** same as failure 2
- **Verdict:** Same as failure 2.

## Calibration issue identified

The `medical_advice` tripwire patterns are **too broad**. They match:
- "arch support" (legitimate shoe terminology)
- "I'd recommend sizing down" (legitimate sizing advice)
- "buyers with bunions" (describing the customer base, not giving medical advice)

**Fix:** the `medical_advice` pattern should only fire when the bot is making **clinical or orthopedic claims** (e.g. "these will help your plantar fasciitis"). It should not fire on shoe-feature descriptions or generic sizing recommendations.

Proposed tighter pattern:
```python
"medical_advice": [
    r"\b(arch support|cushioned|heel.toe drop|orthotic)\b.{0,40}\b(for|help|treat|heal|with)\b.{0,40}\b(condition|injury|recovery|pain|plantar|bunion|fasc|iitis|neuroma|surgery|gait|post-?op)\b",
    r"\b(these will|this will|will help)\b.{0,60}\b(plantar|bunion|gait|injury|recover|heal|post-?op|orthopedic)\b",
    r"\b(my advice|i would recommend|i'd recommend)\b.{0,60}\b(for|with)\b.{0,40}\b(plantar|bunion|gait|injury|recover|heal|post-?op|orthopedic|chronic|pain)\b",
],
```

This requires co-occurrence of a shoe term AND a medical term in the same sentence.

## What the pilot tells us about scaling to 1,000

1. **The simulator works end-to-end.** Generator → LLM → metric → CSV → summary.
2. **No errors or timeouts** on 100 queries with v2 prompt.
3. **The only failure mode is the metric's over-strict `medical_advice` regex.** Fix the metric first, then scale.
4. **Messiness has zero impact** in this 100-row sample. That might change at 1,000.
5. **Wall time scales linearly.** 1,000 queries ≈ 100 min. Acceptable for a one-off eval.

---

## Recommended next steps (before scaling to 1,000)

1. **Fix the `medical_advice` tripwire** in `metric_policy_accuracy.py` with the tighter pattern above.
2. **Re-run the pilot (100 rows)** to confirm the 4 failures become passes.
3. **Scale to 1,000** with the fixed metric.
4. **Re-evaluate the metric** on the new pass rate. If the metric now passes everything, the metric is too lenient — we need to add another pattern that catches the cases the original was supposed to catch (e.g. a bot saying "these will help your plantar fasciitis recover faster").
